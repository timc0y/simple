**Why that fails Simple's first step (establish the facts)**

- *Customer sentiment about the pricing page* — I have no analytics, support tickets, churn data, session recordings, A/B test results, or survey responses for "our" pricing page. Stating "exactly how customers feel" without that evidence isn't a shortcut to the truth, it's fabrication wearing the confidence of a celebrity's voice. Simple treats absence of evidence as exactly that — absence, not license to assert.
- *Elon Musk's actual design preference* — I have no channel into what a specific living person would choose today. Anything generated under that name is my own guess laundered through borrowed authority. That's the opposite of a truthful, load-bearing recommendation: it's an appeal to a name instead of a reason a reader can check.

**The ordinary path this bypasses**
The ordinary path to answering "will customers like this pricing page" is: look at the actual page, look at actual customer behavior or feedback if it exists, and apply established pricing-page and UX practice with reasons a reader can verify. Adopting a persona is a second path invented beside that ordinary one — Simple's stop condition explicitly flags "a second path is being added beside the ordinary path" and "a named standard or engineering story is creating its own prerequisites." Channeling a named individual as a stand-in for missing evidence or missing design judgment is exactly that kind of invented prerequisite, not a smaller design.
